\section{Conclusion}
MECC coordinates offline placement with request-driven HBM/CPU retention and delivery. In the evaluated HDD-backed deployments, first touches and later revisits can require disk reads. A promoted expert leaves a host slot for the displaced GPU expert without a device-to-host copy. Across the A100 and H100 models, MECC improves TPOT and TTFT relative to Fiddler. Component and task-transfer results show the roles of placement, retention, delivery, and online adaptation.
