# R4 — Independent simulated review: presentation and argument

This is a simulated DAC-style review, not a review by an actual committee member. The scale used here is explicitly simulated: 1 reject, 2 weak reject, 3 borderline, 4 accept, 5 strong accept. It is not a verified official DAC scoring scale.

Scope: cold read of the supplied manuscript.tex, manuscript.pdf, and assumptions.json only. I inspected the rendered body pages. I did not inspect other reviews, implementations, evidence files, or external references. Statements about related systems below assess the manuscript's own account, not independently verified literature.

## Recommendation

**Current submission: 1/5, reject.** Every outcome, repeat variation, memory peak, and quality result is explicitly hypothetical. The disclosure is commendably clear, but the draft provides no measured evidence for a systems-paper acceptance decision.

**Conditional technical merit: 2/5, weak reject**, assuming only that the displayed numerical values were measured correctly. This does not assume that planned experiments pass, that local adapters reproduce external systems, or that absent controls have favorable outcomes. The displayed results would establish useful improvements over local baselines. They would still leave the central novelty and attribution questions unanswered.

**Presentation quality considered separately: approximately 4/5.** Most sentences are precise, fluent, and easy to read. The rendered layout is legible and professional. This presentation score is not an overall acceptance recommendation. My confidence is high on language and figure issues and moderate on technical novelty, because this review deliberately excludes implementation and external literature verification.

## Summary and strengths

MECC initializes a three-tier expert cache from an independent route profile, adjusts residency through promotion and demotion, and overlaps transfers with computation. The intended deployment has insufficient memory for complete residency and therefore incurs SSD traffic. The paper carefully distinguishes matched single-GPU comparisons from a different-resource resident reference.

The strongest writing choices are the separation of static placement from runtime behavior, the narrow scope of the static placement proposition, explicit charging of mirrors and staging buffers, and the refusal to present local adapters as reproductions of named systems. The eight-GPU comparison correctly states equal nominal GPU-seconds per token at its highlighted point rather than claiming energy or monetary savings. The paper also distinguishes the 12% controlled-trace miss target from the 20% end-to-end target. These qualifications materially improve interpretability.

## Main reasons the conditional recommendation remains below accept

1. **The contribution is described as a collection of familiar mechanisms rather than a demonstrated new systems insight.** The introduction lists a compiler, runtime, and evaluation, but does not identify which technical decision goes beyond popularity placement, exclusive multilevel LRU, and event-based overlap. The paper itself identifies related work using layer allocation, profile initialization, and runtime routing history. The reader needs a precise account of the remaining difference and evidence that it matters. Calling the planner a compiler does not supply that distinction.

2. **The comparison evidence is weaker than the headline.** The approximately 3× result is against a deliberately unoptimized streaming stack. The more informative improvement is 1.25–1.42× against a local SSD-native adapter, whose omitted mechanisms could affect the ranking. Correctly measured local-adapter numbers would still not establish competitiveness against the cited systems. A mechanism matrix and at least one faithful, relevant matched implementation are needed, or the paper must explicitly narrow its contribution to a controlled mechanism study.

3. **Several controls are promised but not displayed.** Section II says an exclusive global-LRU reference controls for combined capacity, but Figure 2(b) shows only fixed CPU, independent LRU, and joint retention. Section II-C describes a profile/random × static/adaptive crossing, but Figure 2(c) has only three curves. Section V lists physical transfer traffic, duplicate bytes, profile mismatch, no-shift controls, and component interactions without showing their outcomes. Measurement of the currently displayed values would not fill these gaps.

4. **The schedule is insufficiently specified to assess the contribution.** “Known upcoming transfers” and “independent work” do not explain when expert IDs become available, how far ahead delivery can proceed without changing routing, what work overlaps with a miss at batch one, or how in-flight objects interact with eviction. A small dependency timeline and a concrete request example would clarify both implementability and causal interpretation of the overlap result.

5. **Deployment feasibility and usefulness need a clearer argument.** The highlighted single-GPU point takes eight seconds per token and saves no nominal GPU-time against the highlighted eight-GPU point. A 128-token decode at that TPOT is roughly 17 minutes, before other request costs. This can be useful for some workloads, but “applications whose latency allowance permits this tradeoff” is too general. Define a representative offline use case or an explicit latency/resource objective. The existing two-/four-/eight-GPU frontier is more informative than the abstract's isolated eight-GPU contrast.

## Concrete prose and argument edits

- **Abstract, first sentence:** “Single-GPU mixture-of-experts inference requires SSD reads when the expert pool exceeds the available HBM and host working set.” This conflates capacity, occupancy, and compulsory traffic. State the available expert-residency capacities, account for other allocations, and distinguish compulsory cold reads from repeated demand reads. Suggested wording: “We study single-GPU MoE inference under task memory caps that prevent the expert pool from remaining resident in HBM and host memory.” Then explain that SSD is the backing store and that the objective is to reduce repeated delivery stalls.
- **Section II-A:** “We verify this condition through cold-read traffic.” Cold reads alone do not prove that the pool cannot fit; a model that fits can also be read from SSD during initialization. Replace this with a byte-based capacity inequality and separately report post-initialization SSD traffic. Define whether “cold-read” means any SSD demand miss or only a first access; the current use risks confusion with compulsory cache misses.
- **Abstract, results:** The 3× headline promotes the weakest baseline, while the more relevant cache-baseline result appears only in the introduction. For the current design draft, use “The illustrative targets specify…” rather than “Hypothetical results show…”. For a measured version, lead with the strongest justified matched comparison, identify its baseline, and give the memory regime in the same sentence.
- **Introduction, contributions paragraph:** Replace the module inventory with one technical contribution sentence and its boundary. For example, explain exactly what the planner/runtime interface enables that the cited coordinated-residency systems do not. Do not invent this difference; if it cannot be substantiated, narrow the novelty claim.
- **Section III-A:** Define how per-layer normalized probabilities enter the global objective. Each layer's Zipf distribution sums to one, whereas layers can select different numbers of experts per token. Explain whether p_e is a per-selection probability, an expected request count, or a globally normalized frequency. Also explain why the implemented marginal-coverage heuristic omits delivery-cost and size weighting from its slot score, or state the assumptions under which those terms are constant.
- **Section III-B:** “In the controlled object model” is too vague for the global-LRU equivalence claim. State the required transition order, corresponding global stack, tier capacities, and exclusion of prefetch or alternative admission. A short invariant would make the precise scope readable without turning this into a second full theorem.
- **Section V-A, streaming baseline:** “It uses demand-driven staging with profile placement, dynamic CPU retention, and overlap disabled.” The scope of “disabled” is ambiguous and can be read as enabling profile placement and retention. Replace with: “It uses demand-driven staging. Profile-guided placement, dynamic CPU retention, and transfer/computation overlap are disabled.” State what default placement and residency remain enabled.
- **Section V-A, cache baselines:** “RF” is never expanded on first appearance. Use “recency/frequency (RF) adapter,” and give the actual admission/replacement rule. “SSD-native” also needs a concrete policy description rather than a broad inspiration label.
- **Section V-D:** “The placement comparison varies only initialization” does not match the earlier allocation motivation without further explanation. Separate the layer-capacity allocation experiment from the initial expert-ID placement experiment and name what each holds fixed.
- **Section V-E:** “A full request-cost analysis additionally includes…” should remain a limitation, not a claim that the request break-even has been established. The displayed nine-token intersection applies only to the selected initialization-plus-decode terms.
- **Throughout Sections IV–V:** Present-tense statements such as “GPU tests check execution and synchronization” can imply completed implementation validation. State clearly whether this is implemented instrumentation, a proposed test protocol, or completed testing. The blanket hypothetical-result warning does not resolve the implementation-status ambiguity.
- **Terminology:** Prefer “task host-memory cap” to “task CPU budget” when the quantity is memory. Define “miss share” once with its denominator and aggregation rule. Replace slash-separated sequences of four results with a compact table or explicitly map them to model/workload order.

## Figures and captions

- **Figure 1, overview:** Readable, but it visually depicts SSD as holding only e5 and e7, whereas the text says SSD retains all backing weights. Label the tier “SSD backing store: all experts,” and distinguish unique primary residency from backing copies. The arrows from panel (a) through (b) to (c) also risk suggesting that the current online route is the independent profiling input; show offline compilation and online execution as separate paths.
- **Figure 2(a):** The caption says allocation is compared, but the chart shows miss percentages only. Add the uniform/planned slot allocations and the total-capacity equality. Define whether each percentage is per-group demand weighted, and show the weighted aggregate. Equal total slots alone cannot establish equal physical bytes when expert sizes differ.
- **Figure 2(b):** Add the stated exclusive global-LRU reference or remove the claim that it has already controlled for combined capacity. Include duplicate bytes and total charged bytes, because the text explicitly proposes those measurements as the way to distinguish reuse from duplication effects. A caption identifying model/trace, capacity, and denominator would make this panel independently interpretable.
- **Figure 2(c):** Mark the shift with a label rather than an unexplained vertical line, identify requests/tokens per window, and include all four initialization/adaptation combinations if the paper claims the crossed experiment. Show or separately report a no-shift condition. A designed synthetic shift cannot by itself support a claim about natural routing drift.
- **Figure 3:** This is the clearest mechanism figure. Add a brief indication that CPU residency removal occurs before the HBM victim is inserted, and keep the separate mirror accounting explicit.
- **Figure 4(a):** The panel is readable, but the authored ±3% bars imitate empirical repeat variation without contributing evidence. For a design-target artifact, omit error bars and use point targets or ranges explicitly labeled as assumed. In the measured version, define the actual statistic, repeat protocol, and sample size. Include the exact host cap and expert allowance in the caption.
- **Figure 4(b):** Lines connecting categorical model/workload combinations imply continuity. Grouped points or bars would be clearer. “7B-Q” and “7B-C” do not exactly match the “8x7B / QA” labels in panel (a). Use consistent names and say that lower latency is purchased with a different GPU allocation directly in the panel subtitle.
- **Figure 5:** The monotone cumulative ablation supports gains within one insertion order, which the prose correctly acknowledges. Show leave-one-out controls before attributing an independent benefit to each mechanism. Captions should name the model/workload, HBM/host budgets, and whether initialization is excluded.
- **Figure 6(a):** “Task CPU budget” should be “Task host-memory cap.” Identify the fixed HBM allowance and workload in the caption.
- **Figure 6(b):** “One-GPU residency does not fit” is ambiguous because MECC also has residency. Use “The full-model-resident reference exceeds one GPU's memory capacity.” Explain the multi-GPU extension of MECC, whose main design is introduced as single-GPU.
- **Figure 6(c):** The title “Include the cost of the prior” is stronger than what is plotted. Profile-generation cost is explicitly excluded. Rename it “Initialization-plus-decode illustration,” identify what the 30 s and 12 s include, and label the nine-token intersection. Avoid calling this full startup amortization.

The body fits cleanly into five pages, with references on a separate sixth page in the supplied PDF. Figure labels remain legible at the rendered size. Figures 5 and 6 occupy much of the final body page, so their captions must carry more experimental context than they currently do. Language polishing alone will not repair the missing comparisons.

## What would justify 4/5

An accept recommendation would require measured results with frozen workloads and revisions; a precise, substantiated novelty statement against the closest coordinated-residency work; faithful relevant baselines under the same charged physical memory caps; and the missing controls needed to connect the gains to the proposed mechanisms. At minimum, show the global-LRU comparison, initialization/adaptation crossing, exposed-transfer/physical-traffic measurements, realistic profile costs, and quality/synchronization results. These requirements do not presume favorable outcomes.

For presentation, the highest-value revision is to center one falsifiable thesis: what specific compiler/runtime coordination improves which cost under which memory constraint, beyond which concrete baseline. Then make the abstract, mechanism diagrams, baseline definitions, and main results answer that same question. The current draft is already readable enough that this argument-and-evidence revision, rather than more ornate English, determines whether it can reach 4/5.
